Dubious Experiments · No. 3

Clickbait is kak, so I built Just Tell Me, Bru!

Liam Kloppers · 8 October 2026 · 2 min read

I asked my friend “What’s more painful? Clickbait headlines or passing kidney stones?” He replied “Let’s put it this way, a clickbait headline has never made me cry myself to sleep at night.”

That’s not really the answer I wanted going into this article. I haven’t had kidney stones, but every time I see a clickbait article it’s at least the mental equivalent of stubbing my toe on a coffee table.

Two South African websites owned by the same company (Broad Media) leave me with this feeling: MyBroadband and BusinessTech. If you want informative roundups about tech, business and current events in South Africa, they’re a great place to get that. But they’re basically a Rube Goldberg machine. I click a vague headline and after a series of ups and downs, the marble lands on a pink cushion instead of doing a backflip and cracking the egg.

Here are some examples:

Oh good news! What could it be? Better interest rates on savings accounts? More rewards partners?

Oh no, it’s just a name change.

Wow, a 48-hour warning? This must be urgent!

Oh no, the 48 hours was quoted as a critical time by a lawyer and isn’t really a thing.

My point is I can barely ever guess the article’s contents without clicking it. The information in the headline isn’t nearly enough, leaving me feeling like I stubbed my toe when I click the article and it was a total waste of time.

Try your hand at it below. I pulled 198 headlines from BusinessTech and MyBroadband and had Claude Sonnet 5.5 try to guess what each article was about. It only got the full story right 27% of the time.

Open the game on its own page

Unless you’re a mind-reader, you got some wrong.

I want my headlines informative. I don’t want to search for them inside a Kinder Egg. So Just Tell Me, Bru! It’s an extension I made that rewrites these headlines into less clickbaity alternatives. The screenshots I used above are actually the extension in action.

It also turns “five things you need to know” roundups into a list of what’s actually in them:

And the site’s original headline stays underneath in small grey text, or you can switch it off:

Get Just Tell Me, Bru! on the Chrome Web Store. It’s free.

There’s more about it, including the privacy policy, at bru.011.sh.

Clickbait is kak and we don’t have to deal with it.
